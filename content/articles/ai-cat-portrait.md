## Photo tips that matter more for cats than “just upload”

Phone photos are fine. Filters are not. The homepage FAQ is blunt: **photo quality affects results**; good light, a visible face, and a file that is not a blur. The generator will not magically recover a black silhouette or a mid-pounce smear. For a photo that is already a lost cause, use the [blurry pet photo guide](https://pixpawai.com/en/blog/blurry-pet-photo-to-stunning-ai-art-guide/) rather than repeating that tutorial here.

**General (every cat):**

- Face unobstructed — no paws, no half a sofa, no heavy bokeh over the eyes.
- **Eyes visible.** A squint is better than a closed nap if you have a second shot.
- **One main subject.** Crop the second cat out of the frame instead of hoping the model will sort a pile.
- Skip heavy Instagram filters. They crush orange into brown and black into a void before the generator ever sees the file.
- Shoot closer than a whole-room wide shot. The portrait is a face (and, for patterned cats, the chest or shoulder that carries the pattern).

None of this is a likeness bake-off against other apps. **PixPaw has not been tested here as “best on calicos.”** These are lighting and framing habits that keep markings in the pixels.

### Tabby: keep the stripes readable

A tabby’s likeness is the **stripes** — mackerel lines, blotched swirls, the forehead **“M”** if your cat has one. A dark, motion-blurred pounce shot turns all of that into mud.

- Prefer a **side or three-quarter face** over a straight-down “loaf from the ceiling” crop, unless the M and both eyes are still sharp.
- **Do not crop off the forehead M** if that is how you would recognize the cat on a phone lock screen.
- Hold still. Tabby contrast lives in thin edges; camera shake deletes them.
- Indoor window light from the **side** often shows stripe relief better than a single overhead lamp (which can flatten the coat into one grey).

If the only sharp frame you have is a blurry leap, salvage that file with the blurry-photo guide; do not spend a credit hoping stripes reappear.

### Calico and tortie: the patches *are* the likeness

Calico and tortoiseshell cats are where generic “orange cat” outputs come from. The generator can only keep what the photo still separates.

**Calico** (distinct white / orange / black — or cream — patches):

- **Even lighting** so orange, black, and cream do not collapse into one blob on the chest.
- Include the **chest or shoulder** if that is where the landmark patch lives. A tight face crop of a white blaze with all the orange off-frame is a different cat.
- Avoid mixed color temperature (warm lamp + blue TV) on a calico; the patches start to false-merge.

**Tortie** (mottled / brindled black and orange, usually little white):

- The marbling is finer than a calico’s blocks. You need **enough resolution on the cheek and shoulder** that the brindling is still a pattern, not a brown smear.
- Same even light as calico. A hard shadow across the face will eat half the mottling.

Sit the cat where the light hits the patterned side. One well-lit still frame beats five dramatic under-the-bed candids.

### Black cats: underexposure is the usual fail

Black fur is the classic “the AI invented a grey void” case — usually because the *photo* was already a silhouette.

- Put **window light or a lamp in front of** the cat, not only behind. A harsh rear silhouette should not be the only file you upload.
- Look for a **catchlight in the eyes**. If you cannot see a specular in the pupil, the face is probably underexposed for a portrait.
- Expose for the **fur on the face**, not the bright window. A slightly pale background is more useful than a perfect-exposure wall and a black hole where the cat was.
- A three-quarter angle still shows ear set and cheekbones; a full-front flash can flatten whisker pads and blow the nose.

Results vary. There is **no claim that every black-cat photo “just works,”** and no extra quality guarantee beyond the existing first-generation **“Not quite”** credit refund on the site. If the face is a silhouette, fix the light and shoot again before you generate.

Complex, high-detail jobs can take up to about **60 seconds**. That is processing time, not a promise that the model will invent markings the photo never had.
