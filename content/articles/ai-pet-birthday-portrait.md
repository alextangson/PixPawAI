## Quick photo tips for birthday day

You do not need a studio. Keep these short and executable:

- **Face clear.** Eyes and nose in frame. If the birthday hat sits low, tilt it up or shoot from a slightly higher angle so the eyes stay visible.
- **Soft light.** Window light or a bright lamp beats harsh flash bounce off frosting. Soft light keeps markings readable in the preview.
- **Cake and props behind or beside — not over the nose.** A cake in the foreground that covers the muzzle is a common party fail. Pull the plate back, or shoot the face first, treat the cake as a side prop.
- **One strong still beats a burst of blur.** Wait for a half-second of calm (or bribe with a treat) rather than chasing motion mid-bark.

If the first upload looks soft or cropped at the ear, swap the photo before you spend credits. Fixing the source is cheaper than repeating a bad crop.

## When a calm pre-party portrait works better

Sometimes the party itself is the wrong set. Reach for a calm shot taken earlier the same day (or the day before) when:

- The room is dark and every frame has flash glare on the eyes
- Guests fill the background and every crop still includes three elbows
- The pet is overstimulated — spinning, hiding, or refusing the hat entirely

A quiet hallway or balcony photo still counts as a birthday keep once you style it. You are capturing *their* face on the occasion, not documenting every balloon. If the live party frames all fail the face test, upload the calm one and pick Birthday Celebration (or a quieter card) so the mood still reads “today,” not “random Tuesday.”
