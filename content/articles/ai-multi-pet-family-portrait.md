## What “multi-pet family portrait” means here

Here, “multi-pet” means **one photo, multiple pet faces → one AI portrait**. You are not stitching three solo generations into a collage by hand. You start with a group shot (or a tight pair) where each animal you care about is visible enough to judge in the preview.

For a household with several pets, the aim is to keep everyone recognizable in the same frame. Check each face and the balance of the whole group before choosing a result.

## Photo basics for two or more pets

You do not need a studio. A phone photo works when a few basics are true:

- **Faces visible.** Eyes and noses in frame for each pet you want to keep. A head buried in a blanket will not magically become a clear portrait.
- **Similar distance to the camera.** One pet three feet closer will dominate; the other may look soft or tiny. Sit them on the same couch plane when you can.
- **Soft, even light.** Daylight near a window or a bright indoor lamp beats a dark hallway with one harsh flash.
- **Less overlap.** Mild shoulder overlap is fine; a full face blocked by another pet’s ear is hard to fix in one generation.

Fix the upload before spending credits on a bad crop. If the free watermarked try already loses one face, swap the photo first.

## When separate shots are smarter

Sometimes one group frame is the wrong first move:

- **One pet always turns away** the moment the phone comes up
- **Huge size mismatch** (toy breed next to a giant) that forces an awkward crop
- **Busy motion** where only one face is sharp

In those cases, generate **strong solo keepers** first, then decide later whether you still want a group try.
