## What each path is good at

Think in lanes, not winners.

**A traditional painter** is strong when you want **original craft**: brushwork you can feel up close, a one-of-one object, and the story of sitting (or sending references) with a working artist. Commission pieces often carry heirloom weight — the kind of object you hang because a human hand decided every edge.

**An AI pet portrait from a photo** is strong when you want **speed and try-before-you-frame**: see a styled likeness tonight, compare a couple of moods, keep a digital file for print-later or a long-distance send. It is a different product category from gallery oil — closer to “preview and decide” than “commission and wait.”

Neither lane cancels the other. You can try AI first to explore likeness and style, then hire a painter later for a formal piece.

## When a traditional painter fits best

Choose (or keep) a painter when you want:

- **One-of-one oil, watercolor, or pastel** meant as a lasting physical object.  
- **Gallery show or formal commission** where provenance and hand matter.  
- **Complex narrative scenes** — multiple pets, a specific room, a story the artist builds with you.  
- **A relationship with the maker** — progress notes, style signature, and a piece that only that hand would finish.

A working artist brings individual judgment, technique, and collaboration to those goals.
